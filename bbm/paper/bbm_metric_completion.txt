== ABSTRACT ==

The Bender--Brody--Muller (BBM) Hamiltonian $H=\Delta^{-1}(\hat x\hat p+\hat p\hat x)\Delta$,
with $\Delta=1-e^{-i\hat p}$, is symmetric for the candidate metric
$\eta_0=\Delta^\dagger\Delta$, whose symbol $4\sin^2(k/2)$ is positive with trivial kernel but
not coercive. We work out the consequences. In the dilation representation any metric diagonal in
momentum becomes a gauge factor along the dilation flow which cancels from both the eigenvalue
equation and the induced normalisation; consequently no such metric can alter the spectrum, which
remains $R$, simple and purely absolutely continuous, and the BBM eigenfunctions
$\psi_z=-\zeta(z,\cdot+1)$ fail to lie in the completion by a logarithmic margin that is exactly
marginal on the critical line.

The zeros of the symbol at $k\in2\pi\Z$ nevertheless cut the line into intervals and open a family
of self-adjoint extensions. The decoupled ones are genuinely discrete above an infrared cutoff and,
unlike compact quantum graphs, reproduce the correct $T\log T$ Weyl law. We show that they still
fail, uniformly in the extension phases: writing $c=T/2\pi$ and $L_n=\log(1+1/n)$,
\[
N_\theta(T)\;\ge\;\sum_{n\ge1}\Big\lfloor \tfrac{T L_n}{2\pi}\Big\rfloor
\;=\;N_{Riemann}(T)+\frac{\gamma}{2\pi} T+o(T)
\qquad\text{for every }\{\theta_n\},
\]
with $\gamma$ the Euler--Mascheroni constant. The bound is the Dirichlet divisor summatory
function in disguise, $\sum_{n}\lfloor cL_n\rfloor=D(c)-\gamma c+o(c)$: the arithmetic this
construction encodes is that of divisors, not of zeros. An independent rigidity obstruction, and
the nearest-neighbour statistics, are quantified numerically. All computations are reproducible
from the accompanying code.

== Introduction ==

Bender, Brody and Muller [BBM2017] proposed, as a candidate Hilbert--P\'olya operator,

\hat H=\frac{1}{1-e^{-i\hat p}} (\hat x\hat p+\hat p\hat x) \bigl(1-e^{-i\hat p}\bigr),
\qquad \hat p=-i d/dx ,

on the half line. Its formal eigenfunctions are Hurwitz zeta functions and the boundary condition
$\psi(0)=0$ reduces to $\zeta(z)=0$, which is a striking and genuine feature of the construction.
The difficulty, identified early [Bellissard2017] and analysed in detail in
[Liu2026], is that the operator is not symmetric on $L^2(0,\infty)$ and the metric proposed
to repair this is degenerate.

This paper does three things. First (sections on coercivity and the dilation representation) it records, with
numerical verification, exactly where and how badly the metric fails, and locates the eigenstates
relative to the completion; this overlaps with [Liu2026] and is included for completeness and
because the quantitative form of the failure is used later. Second
(section on momentum-diagonal metrics) it proves that the failure cannot be repaired by any metric
diagonal in momentum, for a structural reason that is transparent in the dilation representation.
Third, and this is the main contribution (sections on decoupled extensions and the theta-uniform obstruction), it
addresses the one loophole that survives: the zeros of the metric symbol open a family of
self-adjoint extensions which are discrete and which, unlike the compact quantum graphs of
Endres and Steiner [EndresSteiner2010], carry the correct $T\log T$ Weyl law. We show these
fail at the next order, uniformly in their free parameters, by an amount equal to the
Euler--Mascheroni constant.

The distinction from [EndresSteiner2010] is worth stating at once. Endres and Steiner give a
complete classification of the self-adjoint realisations of the Berry--Keating operator on
compact quantum graphs, together with the secular equation, an exact trace formula and the
Weyl asymptotics, and observe that the resulting counting functions are linear in $T$ and
therefore cannot be the Riemann counting function. The graph generated here is not compact: the
symbol's zero set $k\in2\pi\Z$ produces infinitely many edges, of lengths $L_n=\log(1+1/n)$ with
$\sum_nL_n=\infty$, and the counting function is genuinely $T\log T$. The leading-order argument is
therefore unavailable, and the obstruction has to be found one order down.

== Setup and conventions ==

On $L^2(0,\infty,d x)$ put $\Delta=1-e^{-i\hat p}$, so that $(\Delta\psi)(x)=\psi(x)-\psi(x-1)$
with $\psi\equiv0$ for $x<0$, and
\[
A:=\hat x\hat p+\hat p\hat x=-i\Bigl(x\tfrac{d}{d x}+\tfrac{d}{d x}x\Bigr),
\qquad H=\Delta^{-1}A \Delta .
\]
$H$ is symmetric for
$\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle_{\eta_0}=\langle\Delta\psi,\Delta\varphi\rangle$, $\eta_0=\Delta^\dagger\Delta$,
because $\langle\psi,H\varphi\rangle_{\eta_0}=\langle\Delta\psi,A\Delta\varphi\rangle$ and $A$ is
symmetric.

Two unitary changes of variable are used throughout. In position, $u=\log x$ and
$(U\psi)(u)=e^{u/2}\psi(e^u)$ maps $L^2(R_+,d x)\to L^2(R,d u)$ and carries
$A\mapsto-2i d/du$. In momentum, $v=\log k$ and the same substitution carries
$A\mapsto 2i d/dv$. We refer to either as the dilation representation.

We write $\gamma$ for the Euler--Mascheroni constant, $\gamma_n$ for the ordinary part of the
$n$-th nontrivial zero, $c=T/2\pi$, and
\[
N_{Riemann}(T)=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi}-\frac{T}{2\pi}+\frac78
\]
for the smooth Riemann--von Mangoldt counting function.

== The metric is positive, has trivial kernel, and is not coercive ==

$\Delta$ is the Fourier multiplier with symbol $1-e^{-ik}$, hence

\eta_0=f_0(\hat p),\qquad f_0(k)=\bigl|1-e^{-ik}\bigr|^2=4\sin^2(k/2).

Thus $f_0>0$ off $2\pi\Z$, so $\eta_0>0$ with trivial kernel, while $f_0$ vanishes to second order
at every $k\in2\pi\Z$, so $\inf\spec\eta_0=0$: the form is not coercive, and
$\|\cdot\|_{\eta_0}$ is strictly weaker than $\|\cdot\|_{L^2}$.

PROPOSITION. 
Let $\eta_0^{(X)}$ denote $\Delta^\dagger\Delta$ compressed to $(0,X]$ with a Dirichlet condition at
the origin. Then $\lambda_{\min}(\eta_0^{(X)})\sim(\pi/2X)^2$ and
$\sup_\psi\|\psi\|/\|\Delta\psi\|\sim2X/\pi$ as $X\to\infty$.

On a grid of spacing $1/m$ the unit shift is an exact index shift, so $\Delta$ decouples into $m$
chains, each of which is the unit lower bidiagonal matrix; $\Delta^\dagger\Delta$ is then the
discrete Neumann--Dirichlet Laplacian, whose smallest eigenvalue is
$4\sin^2\bigl(\pi/(2(2X+1))\bigr)$. the table below confirms both statements.

$X$  |  $\lambda_{\min}$  |  $(\pi/2X)^2$  |  ratio  |  $\sup\|\psi\|/\|\Delta\psi\|$ vs $2X/\pi$

32   |  $2.3355x10^{-3}$  |  $2.4096x10^{-3}$  |  0.9693  |  $20.69$ / $20.37$
128  |  $1.4943x10^{-4}$  |  $1.5060x10^{-4}$  |  0.9922  |  $81.81$ / $81.49$
512  |  $9.3940x10^{-6}$  |  $9.4124x10^{-6}$  |  0.9980  |  $326.27$ / $325.95$

Table: Failure of coercivity, quantified.

Three consequences matter. (i) The extremal direction is the slowly varying $k\to0$ mode: the
$\eta_0$-norm sees only unit differences and is blind to anything varying slowly on the scale of
the shift. (ii) $\Delta^{-1}=\sum_{n\ge0}e^{-in\hat p}$ is unbounded, so $H=\Delta^{-1}A\Delta$ is
not a bounded similarity transform of $A$ and no spectral statement transfers for free. (iii) The
completion

H_{\eta_0}=\overline{C_c^\infty(0,\infty)}^{ \|\cdot\|_{\eta_0}}
=\{\psi:\Delta\psi\in L^2(0,\infty)\}

is not contained in $L^2$: the constant function already belongs to it. The map
$V:\psi\mapsto\Delta\psi$ is unitary from $H_{\eta_0}$ onto $L^2(0,\infty)$.

== Dilation representation and the missing eigenstates ==

PROPOSITION. 
On $H_{\eta_0}$ one has $VHV^{-1}=A$. In the dilation representation $H$ is multiplication by
the dilation variable; its free self-adjoint realisation has spectrum $R$, simple and purely
absolutely continuous.

This is the content of [Liu2026]; we verified it numerically to $10^{-14}$ by comparing the
box spectrum with the ladder $4\pi n/L_u$.

The generalised eigenfunctions are $A x^{-z}=i(2z-1)x^{-z}$, real eigenvalue iff
$Re z=\frac12$, with $z=\frac12-\frac{iE}{2}$. The BBM eigenfunctions are
$\psi_z(x)=-\zeta(z,x+1)$, and the Hurwitz difference identity $\zeta(z,a)-\zeta(z,a+1)=a^{-z}$
gives exactly

\Delta\psi_z=x^{-z},\qquad \psi_z(0)=-\zeta(z),

so the boundary condition $\psi_z(0)=0$ is $\zeta(z)=0$, with eigenvalue $E=-2\gamma_n$ at
the zero $\tfrac12+i\gamma_n$. Both identities were verified to $10^{-32}$ at $z=\rho_1$.

COROLLARY. 
For every $z$ with $Re z=\frac12$,
$\|\psi_z\|_{\eta_0}^2=\int_0^\infty x^{-2Re z}d x=\int_0^\infty d x/x=\infty$,
so $\psi_z\notin H_{\eta_0}$. Moreover $\psi_z\notin L^2$, since
$\psi_z(x)\sim x^{1-z}/(z-1)$.

Numerically $\int_a^b|\Delta\psi_z|^2d x=\log(b/a)$ to twelve digits. Two features deserve
emphasis. The divergence is only logarithmic and is symmetric between $x\to0$ and $x\to\infty$:
$Re z=\frac12$ is precisely the exponent at which $x^{-z}$ fails square integrability
at both ends simultaneously. And the failure is not repairable by enlarging the space: these are
the delta-normalised generalised eigenfunctions of an absolutely continuous spectrum.

== No momentum-diagonal metric can discretise ==

PROPOSITION. 
Let $\eta=f(\hat p)$ with $f>0$ a.e. and $g=f^{1/2}$. In the momentum dilation representation
$v=\log k$, $A=2i d/dv$, $\eta$ is multiplication by $g(v)^2$, and
\[
H_g=g^{-1}Ag=2i\frac{d}{d v}+2i(\log g)',
\qquad
\psi_E(v)=g(v)^{-1}e^{-iEv/2},
\qquad
\|\psi_E\|_{\eta}=\bigl\|e^{-iEv/2}\bigr\| .
\]
In particular the eigenfunctions and their induced norms are independent of $f$ up to the gauge
factor, and if $0<c_1\le f\le c_2$ then $spec H_g=spec A=R$.

The metric is a pure gauge along the dilation flow; it cancels from the eigenvalue equation and
from the normalisation alike. It can change which states are normalisable, never where the
spectrum lies.

We tested eight symbols numerically --- BBM, a coercive regularisation, $k^2$, $k^4$, $1+k^2$,
$e^{-1/k^2}$, $4\sin^2(k/2)(1+k^2)$ and the trivial one --- with gauge condition numbers ranging
over five orders of magnitude. In every case
$\max|spec H_g-spec A|\le1.4x10^{-11}$ with imaginary parts below $10^{-11}$; the predicted
eigenfunctions gave residuals near $10^{-12}$ and an $\eta$-norm equal to $\sqrt{L_v}$ independent
of the metric. Under box scaling every level slides as $4\pi/\log(\text{box})$, identically for all
symbols, over sixteen orders of magnitude in box size; nothing converges.

A metric diagonal in position fares no better: in $u=\log x$, conjugating $A$ by $x^a$ gives
$H_w=-2i d/du-2ia$, a rigid translation off the real axis, verified pointwise to $10^{-11}$.
Such an $\eta$ is not a metric for $H$ at all.

== Decoupled extensions: discreteness and the Weyl law ==

The symbol $g(k)=2|\sin(k/2)|$ vanishes at $k\in2\pi\Z$. Functions blowing up like $1/g$ there
still have finite $\eta_0$-norm, so each interval $I_n=(2\pi n,2\pi(n+1))$, of length
$L_n=\log(1+1/n)$ in $v$, supports solutions for every real $E$: numerically
$\|\psi_E\|_{\eta_0}^2=L_n$ exactly, for arbitrary real $E$. Hence every real number is an
eigenvalue of the adjoint with infinite multiplicity and the deficiency indices are
$(\infty,\infty)$, as reported in [Liu2026]; the free extension glues the intervals and is
the purely continuous operator of the Proposition above.

Among the extensions that do not couple the intervals, each $I_n$ carries the $U(1)$ family
of quasi-periodic conditions $\psi(b)=e^{i\theta_n}\psi(a)$ --- the complete set of self-adjoint
realisations of a first-order operator on an interval. In the variable $\gamma=E/2$ the levels of
$I_n$ are

\gamma^{(n)}_m=\frac{2\pi m+\theta_n}{L_n},\qquad m\in Z,
\qquad\text{spacing } s_n=\frac{2\pi}{L_n}\sim 2\pi n .

The interval $k\in(0,2\pi)$ has infinite length in $v$ and contributes absolutely continuous
spectrum; we remove it by an infrared cutoff $k>2\pi$. What remains has finitely many levels below
every height and is therefore genuinely discrete, with counting function of order $T\log T$. This
is the point at which a compactness argument of the kind used in [EndresSteiner2010] is no
longer available.

== The theta-uniform obstruction ==

THEOREM. 
Let $N_\theta(T)$ be the number of levels in $(0,T]$ of the decoupled extension with phases
$\{\theta_n\}_{n\ge1}$, $\theta_n\in[0,2\pi)$, and let $c=T/2\pi$. Then for every choice of phases

N_\theta(T)\;\ge\;\sum_{n\ge1}\bigl\lfloor c L_n\bigr\rfloor .

PROOF. 
Fix $n$ and write $a=cL_n$, $t=\theta_n/2\pi\in[0,1)$. The positive levels of the display above
not exceeding $T$ are those with $0<2\pi m+\theta_n\le TL_n$. If $t=0$ this forces $m\ge1$ and
$m\le a$, giving $\lfloor a\rfloor$ levels. If $t>0$ it forces $m\ge0$ and $m\le a-t$, giving
$\lfloor a-t\rfloor+1$ levels. Write $a=A+f$ with $A=\lfloor a\rfloor$ and $f\in[0,1)$. If $t\le f$
then $\lfloor a-t\rfloor=A$ and the count is $A+1$; if $t>f$ then $\lfloor a-t\rfloor=A-1$ and the
count is $A$. In all cases the count is at least $A=\lfloor cL_n\rfloor$. Summing over $n$ gives
the display above.

PROPOSITION. 
With $D(c)=\sum_{n\le c}\lfloor c/n\rfloor$ the Dirichlet divisor summatory function,

\sum_{n\ge1}\lfloor cL_n\rfloor=\sum_{m\ge1}\Bigl\lfloor\frac{1}{e^{m/c}-1}\Bigr\rfloor
=D(c)-\gamma c+o(c).

PROOF. 
The first equality is exact and is the layer-cake identity
$\sum_n\lfloor cL_n\rfloor=\sum_{m\ge1}\#\{n:L_n\ge m/c\}$ together with
$\#\{n:\log(1+1/n)\ge y\}=\lfloor 1/(e^{y}-1)\rfloor$; it was verified exactly for
$c$ up to $10^6$. For the second, set $\delta_n=1/n-\log(1+1/n)$, so that
$cL_n=c/n-c\delta_n$ and $\sum_{n\ge1}\delta_n=\lim_M(H_M-\log(M+1))=\gamma$. Then
\[
\sum_n\bigl(\lfloor cL_n\rfloor-\lfloor c/n\rfloor\bigr)
=-c\sum_n\delta_n+\sum_n\bigl(\{c/n\}-\{cL_n\}\bigr)=-\gamma c+o(c),
\]
the last step requiring that the fractional-part sum be $o(c)$.

REMARK. 
The $o(c)$ in the fractional-part sum is the one analytic gap in this draft. Numerically the
discrepancy between $\sum_n\lfloor cL_n\rfloor$ and $D(c)-\gamma c$ is $76$ at $c=10^6$, i.e.\
consistent with $O(\sqrt c)$, matching the error term in Dirichlet's theorem
$D(c)=c\log c+(2\gamma-1)c+O(\sqrt c)$. Note that the fractional parts are not
equidistributed: $\sum_n\{cL_n\}/c\to1-\gamma=0.422784$, measured $0.422769$ at $c=10^6$, rather
than the $1/2$ equidistribution would give. A rigorous treatment should go through this
statement.

COROLLARY. 
For every choice of extension phases,

N_\theta(T)\;\ge\;N_{Riemann}(T)+\frac{\gamma}{2\pi} T+o(T).

The decoupled extension therefore carries at least $(\gamma/2\pi)T\approx0.0919 T$ levels below
height $T$ that are not zeros, and cannot be made to coincide with the zero set.

PROOF. 
Combine the Theorem above with the divisor identity and
$D(c)=c\log c+(2\gamma-1)c+O(\sqrt c)$, giving
$\sum_n\lfloor cL_n\rfloor=c\log c+(\gamma-1)c+o(c)$, against
$N_{Riemann}(T)=c\log c-c+\frac78$.

the table below shows the convergence; the last column tends to $\gamma=0.577216$. The

at three heights.

== Rigidity and spectral statistics ==

the Corollary above is a statement about density. A second, independent and numerically much
stronger obstruction is that each ladder the display above is an arithmetic progression while the
zeros are not. Since the ladders are decoupled, the joint optimum over phases factorises, and each
$\theta_n$ may be optimised separately. the table below reports the minimum number of levels

The zeros are consistent with GUE and reject Poisson decisively. The two candidate spectra sit at
opposite extremes: the tuned ladder is a perfectly rigid picket fence, and the decoupled extension
shows less rigidity than Poisson, because ladders with commensurable spacings pile levels
together. Matching a counting function is one smooth constraint; neither model carries the
correlations.

== Scope, relation to earlier work, and the open problem ==

the section above overlap with [Liu2026] and are presented as
verification. the Proposition above is elementary once stated in the dilation
representation, but appears not to have been used to rule out the metric-repair strategy as a
class. The $xp$ literature already supplies operators whose spectra reproduce the averaged zeros
[Sierra2008,Sierra2011,BerryKeating1999], so a correct $T\log T$ Weyl law is not by itself
evidence of anything; the Corollary above is a statement one order below that.

the Theorem above and the Corollary above cover the decoupled family only: one
phase per interval, no coupling between intervals. Extensions that couple the intervals form a
strictly larger family. For compact quantum graphs that family has been completely
classified by Endres and Steiner [EndresSteiner2010], who rule it out by a Weyl argument;
their argument does not apply here, because the graph generated by the BBM symbol has infinitely
many edges of total infinite length and its Weyl law is $T\log T$ rather than linear. Whether an
analogue of the display above holds for coupled extensions of this non-compact graph is open, and
is the natural next question. The mechanism behind the Theorem above is the exact
equispacing of the spectrum of a first-order operator on an interval, which coupling destroys: the
secular determinant no longer factorises. A trace formula for the infinite graph, in the spirit of
[EndresSteiner2010], is the obvious tool.

== Numerical methods and reproducibility ==

All computations use NumPy, SciPy and mpmath. Position-space work is done on a uniform grid of
spacing $1/m$, so that the unit shift defining $\Delta$ is an exact index shift and no
interpolation error enters; $A$ is discretised as $-i(X\partial+\partial X)$ with a centred
difference, which is exactly Hermitian. Dilation-space work uses Fourier spectral differentiation
on a periodic grid, reproducing the ladder $4\pi n/L_u$ to $10^{-14}$. The Hurwitz identities are
evaluated with mpmath at 30 digits. Zeros are obtained from mpmath.zetazero. The spacing
statistics unfold the zeros by the Riemann--von Mangoldt smooth counting function and the decoupled
spectrum by a local density estimate on a centred window of $400$ levels.

Code and saved output accompany this draft as step1\_coercivity.py,
step2\_dilation.py, step3\_metric\_scan.py, step4\_zeta\_compare.py and
step5\_rigidity.py.
